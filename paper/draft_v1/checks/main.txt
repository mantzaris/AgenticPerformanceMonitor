    Evidence-Grounded Agentic Dashboards: Numerical Validity, Answer
                Completeness, and Adaptive Presentation




Keywords:       Analytical Agents, Longitudinal Data, Evidence Provenance, Answer Completeness, Adaptive Dashboards.

Abstract:       Longitudinal dashboards must distinguish changes in a person’s recorded activity from differences due to the
                comparison population, time window, or availability of observations. We study a division of responsibilities in
                which an agent requests analyses and selects answers, deterministic tools calculate descriptive quantities, and
                validated evidence drives a controlled compiler and adaptive presentation. A frozen development comparison
                uses 24 people from two Open University course offerings, six question families, deterministic enumeration,
                and three Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct selection interfaces. The interfaces retain authored support metadata, derive
                that metadata, or additionally require explicit analytical identities. Enumeration produced 24/24 complete vis-
                ible answers; the agent interfaces produced 21/24, 15/24, and 15/24, respectively. Completeness attributable to
                model selections was 18/24, 14/24, and 11/24. Explicit identity selection did not improve the frozen endpoint.
                Accepted outputs could contain correct numbers yet omit the requested comparison. Four derived-metadata
                outputs expose an unresolved equivalent-answer scoring limitation; human adjudication and held-out evalu-
                ation remain pending. New renderings of saved cases illustrate adaptive presentation without establishing a
                usability benefit. The findings support separate accounting for numerical integrity, requested-answer coverage,
                and compiler contributions within a bounded task registry. Writing assistance is disclosed (OpenAI, 2026).
