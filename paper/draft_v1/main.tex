\title{Evidence-Grounded Agentic Dashboards: Numerical Validity, Answer Completeness, and Adaptive Presentation}
\author{}
\keywords{Analytical Agents, Longitudinal Data, Evidence Provenance, Answer Completeness, Adaptive Dashboards.}
\abstract{Longitudinal dashboards must distinguish changes in a person's recorded activity from differences due to the comparison population, time window, or availability of observations. We study a division of responsibilities in which an agent requests analyses and selects answers, deterministic tools calculate descriptive quantities, and validated evidence drives a controlled compiler and adaptive presentation. A frozen development comparison uses 24 people from two Open University course offerings, six question families, deterministic enumeration, and three Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct selection interfaces. The interfaces retain authored support metadata, derive that metadata, or additionally require explicit analytical identities. Enumeration produced \EnumVisible/24 complete visible answers; the agent interfaces produced \AVisible/24, \BVisible/24, and \CVisible/24, respectively. Completeness attributable to model selections was \ASelected/24, \BSelected/24, and \CSelected/24. Explicit identity selection did not improve the frozen endpoint. Accepted outputs could contain correct numbers yet omit the requested comparison. Four derived-metadata outputs expose an unresolved equivalent-answer scoring limitation; human adjudication and held-out evaluation remain pending. New renderings of saved cases illustrate adaptive presentation without establishing a usability benefit. The findings support separate accounting for numerical integrity, requested-answer coverage, and compiler contributions within a bounded task registry. Writing assistance is disclosed \cite{openaiCodex}.}
